Pillars of Vedanta Philosophy

The Three Great Acharyas

The intellectual and spiritual foundations of Sanatana Dharma were codified by three luminous preceptors. Discover their life works, philosophical revelations, and associated heritage routes.

Advaita Vedanta (Non-Dualism)

Adi Shankaracharya

c. 788 – 820 CE • Kalady to Kedarnath
"Brahma Satyam Jagan Mithya, Jivo Brahmaiva Naaparah"
Brahman alone is real; the universe is phenomenal; the soul is fundamentally one with the Supreme.

Synthesized the six sacred systems of worship (Shanmata), walked across the subcontinent establishing the four cardinal Amnaya Peethams (Sringeri, Puri, Dwarka, Jyotirmath), and composed immortal commentaries on the Upanishads, Gita, and Brahma Sutras.

Sacred Destinations:
Kalady Varanasi Sringeri Kedarnath Omkareshwar
Explore Shankara Routes
Vishishtadvaita (Qualified Non-Dualism)

Sri Ramanujacharya

1017 – 1137 CE • Sriperumbudur to Srirangam
"Bhakti-Prapatti-Samashrayah"
Through unreserved surrender to Narayana and profound devotion, all souls without distinction attain eternal grace.

Championed social inclusivity by welcoming all into temple sanctums as 'Thirukulathar'. Systematized temple administration across the 108 Divya Desams, established the Melkote Kalyani, and preserved his sacred body (Thirumeni) at Srirangam.

Sacred Destinations:
Srirangam Sriperumbudur Melkote Tirumala Kanchipuram
Explore Ramanuja Routes
Dvaita Vedanta (Dualism / Tattvavada)

Sri Madhvacharya

1238 – 1317 CE • Pajaka to Badarikashrama
"Harir-eva Paro Devas, Tattvam Veda-pratipaditam"
Lord Hari is the supreme transcendent reality. Souls and matter are eternally distinct, serving the Lord in blissful love.

Founded the historic Krishna Matha at Udupi with the Saligrama idol retrieved from Gopichandana at Malpe. Created the biennial Paryaya rotation among the Ashta Mathas that continues unbroken after seven centuries, and ascended to Upper Badari.

Sacred Destinations:
Pajaka Kshetra Udupi Krishna Matha Kanva Theertha Badarikashrama
